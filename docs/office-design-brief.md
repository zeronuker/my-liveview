# Office design brief: "The Garden Studio"

Agreed with the user on 2026-11-13, after many rounds of top-down sketches (version 14 was the one approved). The sketches were drawn as SVG in the chat and are **not stored**; this document is the record. Read `.claude/skills/office-pixel-art/SKILL.md` before drawing anything for it.

## How to read the coordinates

The sketch used "plan units": about 1 unit = 1 game pixel, and the game tile is 16 pixels, so 16 units = 1 tile. A worker is 12 by 16 pixels, so a door about 18 to 24 units wide is about one tile. Rectangles below are `x, y, width, height` in plan units, with x to the right and y down. The building's main block is x 70 to 630 and y 110 to 510 (about 35 by 25 tiles). The CEO suite sits above it at negative y. When the layout is built in `public/layout.js`, snap every edge to the tile grid (the rounding is a small change; keep rooms as close in size as possible).

## The idea

A friendly, open office in a garden. A neutral shell with a few strongly themed rooms, a main street that links the rooms, loud rooms at the front and quiet ones at the back, lots of daylight, plants and warm lighting. The building is stepped, not a square. It has three levels, each with its own floor colour.

## Rules for the build

1. Walls are solid; people walk only through doors, archways and stairs.
2. Every spot must be reachable from the entrance (`src/layout.test.js`).
3. The guardrail edges are not walkable.
4. The art uses only the colours in `public/palette.js`, one ramp per material, light from the top-left (see the skill).
5. Draw things at game size: a worker is 12 by 16 pixels.

## Decisions taken in the design talks

- The tile stays 16 pixels. Detail comes from walls, props, lighting and the garden, not from a bigger tile.
- No printer room. (The server room replaced it. Open question: add a small printer stand somewhere?)
- No receptionist character (the game has no such role).
- The open office has no walls except the lobby wall; the mezzanine has guardrails.
- The terrace floats over the pond; the pond is large.

## Resized hub and lounge (2026-11-13)

The social hub was too dominant. It is now 7 rows (y 20 to 26), the vending area y 27 to 28 (arch to the lounge at row 29), and the lounge and pantry 11 rows (y 30 to 40). The lounge gained a dining corner (four café tables under two pendant lamps on a check rug), a TV corner (a sofa facing a TV on the east wall), a bookshelf with a reading spot, and three new break spots (Lunch, Watching TV, Reading). Bathroom fixtures (urinals, cubicles, sink counters) now reach into the thin wall beside them (`bleed` on an art entry, `withBleed` in `scenery.js`), so no floor shows between them and the wall. Guardrails have a thin post every four pixels between the thick ones.

## Routes (2026-11-13)

`findPath` (`public/walk.js`) is now a weighted search. With a seed, every cell costs up to 0.3 more or less (a hash of the seed and the cell), so each worker and each trip takes a slightly different way of nearly the same length (at most a couple of steps longer). With a `cost` function, the first and last column of every stair three tiles wide or more (the sides, beside the handrails) cost 8 extra (`STAIR_SIDE` in `stage.js`), so people use the middle of a flight: one lane on the 3-wide stairs, two on the CEO's 4-wide ones. A side is still walked on if there is no other way.

## Pets roam further (2026-11-13)

The pets used to set off only to a spot within six tiles, so they hovered round one corner (the dog and chickens covered about half of their part of the garden in ten minutes, and never went up the west strip). Now a pet mostly sets off for anywhere in its part of the garden, at least six tiles away, by a route with a seed of its own (a cat or dog 70 to 75 times in 100, a chicken 35); the rest of the time it takes a short stroll. The roam grid (`roamGrid`) also lets pets onto the front path, the boardwalk and the terrace wherever nothing stands, which joins the lawn to the terrace over the pond. The east strip stays a part of its own (the building, the pond and the deck's rail cut it off), so pets on it keep to it. The pets are shared out: the cat and two chickens start on the west lawn, the dog and a chicken on the east strip. Measured over ten minutes: the cat covers about 80 percent of the east strip, the dog about 70 percent and a chicken about 55 percent of the west side, which is now 370 cells.

## Smooth movement and koi (2026-11-13)

What is drawn glides after what is true (`public/glide.js`): each person and pet keeps a drawn place that follows the true one through a critically damped spring (about a tenth of a second behind), so starts and stops ease, corners round off and nothing jumps from pixel to pixel. It also says how fast the thing is going and how far it has gone: the walking bounce and the walking frames go with the distance gone (a bounce every 0.6 of a tile for people, every half tile for pets), so a step is the same size at any pace. Something that is moving is drawn between screen pixels (with smoothing on); something still is drawn on them, so it stays crisp. A place that is more than two tiles off (a new arrival, instant speed) is a jump and goes there at once. With less motion the glide is off.

Five koi (orange, white and gold) swim in the pond (`public/fish.js`): each picks a place in the water that it can reach in a straight line, turns towards it at no more than 2.2 radians a second, swims at its own pace (slower in a tight turn and on arriving), and now and then rests a few seconds. The water they use (`waterGrid` in `garden.js`) is the pond's water a little in from its bank and not under the terrace or the boardwalk, 80 cells. They are drawn as shapes (a body, a wagging tail, a fin and an eye), turned to their heading, and stand still with less motion or when paused, like the pets.

## The angled view (built 2026-11-14)

A second way to draw the Office: a 2:1 diamond view with light from the top-left, a cut-away building (walls at the back stand tall, walls facing the viewer are cut low) and upright people, in the style of the user's reference screenshot. Everything in it reads the plan in `public/studio.js`; the flat view is unchanged. All of it uses the palette in `public/palette.js`.

**What is on screen.** Diamond floors in each room's colour, with a lighter pool under each ceiling lamp. A brick slab under the building. Walls with a skirting board, windows with curtains, and doors (see below). Every kind of furniture has its own drawing. The garden: the pond (two ripple frames), its stone bank, the front path, flower beds, grass tufts and flowers, trees, bushes, rocks, and a post-and-rail fence with a gate. The CEO suite stands one storey up, and the lobby, open office and entrance a third of that (the mezzanine). People, pets, name tags, bubbles, confetti and clicking a worker all work as in the flat view. Zoom, pan, Fit and the zoom buttons work too; switching views starts the zoom again.

**The files** (all in `public/`; each new file also needs a line in the `files` list of `src/server.js`, or the server answers 404 and the page breaks):
- `iso.js`: the geometry. A tile is a diamond `tile` wide and half as tall; `screenAt` turns a place on the plan into a place on the screen; `isoExtent` is the size of the whole plan, so the flat view's `fit`, `zoomAt` and `keepInView` work unchanged. Also the levels: `STOREY` (24 pixels, the first floor; a wall at the back stands 16), `MEZZANINE` (8 pixels), `levelHeight`, `stairsOf` (each staircase with the heights of the floors at its two ends), `entrancesOf` (the steps down outside the front doors), `stepHeight` and `liftAt` (how high someone standing at a point of the plan is raised).
- `iso-draw.js`: the small drawing tools: a crisp diamond, boxes, thin upright slabs along a tile's edge, tables. Whole pixels only.
- `iso-art.js`: the world (which tiles are floor, wall, guardrail, and how high each stands), the floors, the slab, the podium, walls (their height, windows), the stairs, chairs and desks, monitors, and `lightsOf`, which says what blinks on a tile.
- `iso-furniture.js`: one drawer for each kind of furniture (`PIECES`), and the lights on them (`LIGHTS`). A drawer paints one tile of a piece and is told which tile of the piece it is.
- `iso-doors.js`: doors. `iso-garden.js`: the garden's ground and the fence; it reuses the flat view's upright pictures of trees, bushes and rocks.
- `iso-scene.js`: paints the ground twice (the pond's two frames) and cuts everything that stands up into bands, one for each diagonal row of tiles (x + y the same), which is the order things are drawn in, back to front. It also paints the hanging lamps and gives `office.js` what it needs to draw them.
- `office.js`: in angled mode `centre()` places everything through the projection (and the lift). The people and pets are put into the same rows as the walls and furniture, so a wall or desk in front hides their feet. The lamps are drawn over everyone, and the koi are drawn under the projection so they lie flat on the water.
- `sprites.js`: two new 3/4 views for people (below).
- `angled-concept.html` (served at `/angled-concept.html`): a static page with the whole office, the open office, and galleries of every furniture kind, every door and every pose from four directions, drawn with the same code as the Office. It is the quickest place to look at a piece of angled art up close.

**Rules for the drawing.** A wall is tall (16) when floor lies behind it (to its east or south), cut low (3) when floor lies only in front, and half height (8) between rooms. A piece of furniture is drawn a tile at a time, back to front, so the neighbours in a piece cover each other's inner faces; only the outer faces and what stands on top need care. The flat art for trees, bushes, rocks and the ceiling lamps is reused as it is (upright pictures work in both views).

**Doors** (`iso-doors.js`). Each door works out which way its wall runs and how tall it is. Swing doors have a wooden leaf standing open at one jamb with a handle; double doors a leaf at each end; sliding doors glass panels at the ends and a small box with a blinking sensor light; arches stone posts, and a lintel only under a tall wall. Each has a strip on the floor. The plan has no swing side, so a swing leaf is hinged on the side the flat view mirrors it to (`door.x % 2`).

**Lights.** The blinking patterns are the flat view's (`public/lights.js`): rack LEDs, the test machine, the vending machines' sign and button, the coffee machine, the side-table lamp, a glint sweeping along every window pane, and the ten hanging lamps. With less motion they hold still. The TVs show a colour-cycling picture, all strips of one TV in step (a light can have a `phase`); the plan's TVs face away from the viewer, so the picture is drawn on the face the viewer sees.

**The levels** (`iso.js`, `makeWorld` in `iso-art.js`). The CEO suite is raised one storey (24 pixels) and the lobby, open office and entrance 8 pixels (the mezzanine; a platform hides the ground behind it, four pixels for each pixel it is high, so a higher one would hide people on the main street just north of the open office). Floors, walls, guardrails, furniture, doors, lamps and lights are drawn that much higher, in the same back-to-front rows as everything else, so a raised floor hides what is behind it. Under each raised edge that faces the viewer stands a plaster podium with a brick base. A wall between floors of different heights (such as the one the suite shares with the back rooms) stands from the ground up to the higher floor and a low rail. A guardrail stands on the edge of the higher floor, and one beside a staircase rises with it. Guardrails on wall cells are drawn as guardrails, not as walls. Each staircase takes the heights at its two ends from the levels it joins: the CEO stairs are six steps (24 pixels), stairs A and B rise 8 pixels; a step has a lit edge, a riser (dark if the step is tall, lighter if low) and a tread, with the carpet runner on the CEO stairs. Outside the front doors there are two steps down to the path. Anyone is drawn at the height of their floor, and someone on a staircase or the entrance steps rises or sinks smoothly. The suite stands north of the back rooms (that is how the plan is laid out), so it reads as a raised wing.

**People.** The flat view's sprites are used, with two new 3/4 views in `sprites.js`: `front3q` (turned towards the lower right) and `back3q` (towards the upper right), each with its own head for short and long hair; the torso and legs come from the side and back views, so every pose works. Going to the lower right or lower left shows `front3q` (flipped for the left), and going to the upper right or upper left shows `back3q`. Poses that only make sense facing you (a raised hand, cheering) stay front-on. The manager keeps the tie and glasses.

**Tests.** `src/iso.test.js` (the projection, a drawer for every kind of furniture, lights, doors, the storey), `src/iso-garden.test.js`, and the sprite tests (every view of every pose is a clean 12 by 16 grid; the angled mapping).

**Checked, and not.** Watched in the browser with the mock sessions (busy-office, long-session, full): the whole angled Office with a crowd, zoom and pan, clicking a worker, the pets, the koi, the lights, the CEO suite and its stairs, with no console errors. Not checked: the other themes and a narrow window, a real Claude session (only mocks), the mezzanine's podium and stairs close up in every corner, and long-run performance (the browser pane drew about 30 frames a second in both views, so no difference between them was measured). Nobody was caught half way up the stairs by eye; the lift is covered by a test.
